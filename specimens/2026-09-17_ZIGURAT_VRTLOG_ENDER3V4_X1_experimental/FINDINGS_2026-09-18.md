# Finding, 2026-09-18 — the terraces of this object carry no weld nodes

Semir opened the A2L file in Bambu Studio and looked at layers 164 and 165 (z 39.36 and 39.60), the first two
layers of terrace 1, and said: two identical layers stacked, which in WEFT should never happen — each layer must
cross the one below so the crossings become weld nodes.

He is right, and it is a design error of mine, not a rendering artefact. The terrace schedule in
`core/weft_ziggurat_geometry.mjs` is `radial, radial, spiral4, fine, spiral8, diamond`: the first two layers are
the same set of radial chords, laid twice. I wrote them that way deliberately — the note in the generator says
"the second layer lies on the first" — reasoning about support and forgetting what the weave is for. Two
coincident layers make no crossings at all: instead of a lattice with nodes, that is one thread deposited twice
into the same place, which fuses into a single thicker strand. The same happens on terrace 3 of the A2L object
and terrace 2 of the Ender object, where the schedule ends `spiral8, fine` after an earlier `fine` layer.

Extent: every terrace of both X1 objects. The walls, the roof grid and the foundation are unaffected — the roof
alternates X and Y directions and does cross, and the walls are the ordinary chord/web weave.

The gate did not and could not catch this: it judges whether deposited material has something beneath it, not
whether consecutive layers intersect. Nothing in the record checks for weld-node formation between typed paths;
that is a gap in the gate worth its own entry.

This file does not change anything else in this folder. The object stands as it was built and recorded; the
verdicts in `manifest.json` and the gate reports are unchanged and still true as far as they go. The method was
replaced rather than repaired: the X2 pair of 2026-09-18 makes its terraces by cutting the corners at 45° and
growing the cut a little per layer, so consecutive chords cross by construction and the terrace is woven.
