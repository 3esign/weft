# OBRTAJ — predictions before printing

Status: designed and generated; no new physical result. Seven wall levels, six horizontal terraces and a closed patterned roof. This is an exploratory sculpture, not a controlled machine comparison with RAZMAK.

The visual idea is a turning sequence interrupted by thin, level ledges. Rounded square → hexagon → ellipse → rounded square → hexagon → ellipse → hexagon changes both the plan and its direction. The first circular terrace approaches 190 mm diameter, projecting beyond the smaller basal frame. Later platforms move outward where needed and return inward. Nominal height is 220 mm; the machine profile is 220 × 220 × 235 mm, leaving 15 mm on all four bed sides and above the object.

## Carrying structure

Only the lowest perimeter frame touches the bed. Each new wall starts on a twelve-layer horizontal woven terrace, 2.4 mm thick. Its footprint stays fixed through those layers. The return count follows the tightest inner spacing; rounded tips avoid the sharp reversal crowding found during generation. A quarter-cell phase change produces actual intersections between neighbouring layers. Walls vary their grammar and chord/web rhythm; the fifth wall uses staple after the perp variant produced local self-overlaps during review.

The outer return is a hypothesis about restraint/counterweight, not a demonstrated stabilizer. Gate-supported endpoints describe the nominal path, not the mechanics of a hot, projecting loop. The Ender bead is still the profile's assumed 0.42 mm, not a new caliper measurement.

## Predictions and observations that can disprove them

| Region | Prediction before printing | Useful contrary observation |
|---|---|---|
| Terrace 1, Z 32–34.4 mm | The round overhang outside the square base and the offset hexagon above make inner and outer behaviour separately visible. | Free tips may curl before the next wall loads the surface. Separate this from later settling. |
| Terrace 2, Z 65.4–67.8 mm | The offset ellipse changes the locations of short and long carrying paths. | Similar nominal spans may sag differently on different sides; record direction and chronology before attributing it to pattern. |
| Terrace 3, Z 97.8–100.2 mm | A square beginning over a curved plan should reveal sensitivity near its corner landings. | The terrace may appear formed but lose contact when a corner's wall starts. Photograph before and after that wall. |
| Terrace 4, Z 129.2–131.6 mm | Changing the rotation direction can move the region of largest deflection rather than simply accumulating twist. | If deflection keeps the same world direction, cooling, motion direction or the earlier deformed support may matter. |
| Terrace 5, Z 158.6–161 mm | Shorter nominal spans may help, but they inherit all deformation below. | A smaller upper terrace that performs worse is evidence against a span-only explanation. |
| Terrace 6, Z 187–189.4 mm | The smallest upper wall challenges whether earlier errors accumulate into a displaced crown. | Record top-centre offset relative to the basal centre, separately from terrace sag. |
| Closed roof, Z 215–218.6 mm; relief to 220 mm | Alternating coarse-to-dense grids will nominally close the top, then carry a seven-lobed growing spiral wave. | Roof holes, hanging strands, scraped relief or additional settling during the drawing distinguish the process that actually occurred. |

## Unknowns and next iteration

Unknown: useful outer/inner return ratio, bonding at crossings, thermal tension, the first-deposition limit of a loop, cumulative deformation through six terraces, response to changed chord/web rhythms, and actual Ender bead width. No strength or numerical sag estimate is claimed. Several design variables change together; the object is meant to locate difficult regions, not to identify a single causal variable in one print.

Record the exact G-code hash, material/batch, temperature or speed changes, elapsed time, interventions and interruption height. Keep fixed side views with a scale before each next wall, after that wall and at completion; identify +X/−X/+Y/−Y. Photograph the underside after removal. Keep a failed or partial object as evidence. A later comparison should hold geometry and settings fixed and vary one routing choice.

Honest verdict: the intended carrying paths, test regions and failure observations are specified. The digital gate cannot determine whether the hot extrusion remains horizontal or whether the proposed outer return reduces sag.
