# DOMET2 — predictions, and what would disprove them

Status: designed and generated; no physical result.

| terrace | prediction | the observation that would change the next design |
|---|---|---|
| T1 chord march | Holds further than any hairpin terrace, because no run has a free end. If it holds past a 16 mm chord it extends the only evidenced number this project has. | If it fails at a chord well **under** 16.2 mm, then a bridge inside a growing lattice is weaker than a bridge between two walls, and the evidenced ceiling does not transfer. |
| T2 speed | A monotonic threshold: clean at 6–12 mm/s, degrading above it. This would explain the whole A2L/Ender difference of 23 September in one variable. | If every sector looks the same, speed is **not** the explanation and layer height or cooling is, which sends the next test to a layer-height ladder. |
| T3 crossing angle | Sector 0 (identical stacked layers) is visibly worse than the rest; above roughly 30° the difference disappears. | If sector 0 looks no worse, then the defect Semir found twice costs nothing mechanically and the rule can be relaxed to a phase rule instead of an angle rule. |
| T4 counterweight | Honestly uncertain, and that is why it is here. The mechanism would be a moment about the wall rim, so any benefit should grow with the outward arm and be visible as less droop on the inward side. | If the inward side droops the same at 0 mm and 26 mm of outward arm, the counterweight hypothesis is dead and the outward projection is decoration that costs thread. If droop gets **worse** with a longer arm, the arm is loading the rim rather than balancing it. |
| T5 thickness | A knee: two or three layers too thin to carry anything, little gained past about eight. | If the thin sectors are indistinguishable from the thick ones, every terrace built so far has been three times thicker than it needed to be. |
| T6 pitch | Fine pitches fuse into a membrane and behave well; coarse ones behave as separate teeth and curl. | If the coarse sectors do better, the failure is thermal (too much material too close) rather than structural. |
| T7 + crown | The grid closes over the seat ring and the rose draws cleanly on a flat surface. | A rose that scrapes, or a crown that forms only after hanging strands accumulate, is a different mechanism from the intended planar progression. |

## How to read it

Sectors ascend clockwise from the shortest rung of each ladder. Photograph each terrace from a fixed side
position with a scale, before the wall above it starts and after. Identify +X/−X/+Y/−Y so a sector can be named
from a photograph. Photograph the underside after removal — the growth cone and any sag show there and nowhere
else. Weigh the object; the header predicts its mass, and on the Ender the bead is a guess, so a large
discrepancy is itself information.

Record per terrace: **the sector of first curl, and the sector where the surface is lost.** Two numbers per
terrace, fourteen numbers for the object. That is the whole result.

Keep a failure. An interrupted DOMET2 is worth more than a finished object that says nothing.

Honest verdict: the test regions and the falsifying observations are specified. A digital gate cannot say
whether hot plastic stays horizontal.
