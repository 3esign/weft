# KRAK — predictions, written before the print

Stated so that the print can contradict them.

1. **K3 holds all the way to 48 mm, and comes out visibly flatter than anything DOMET
   produced.** If this is wrong it is the most useful result on the object, because it
   means corbelling has a limit that is not about the per-layer step.

2. **K2 breaks between 4.5 and 9 mm of advance, and closer to 4.5 than to 9**, because
   the gate measures geometry and the material is weaker than the geometry. Expect 1.5
   and 3.0 clean, 4.5 marginal, 6.0 and 9.0 drooping or tangling.

3. **On K1 the finished order will NOT match the gate's order.** The gate ranks
   `splay` < `truss` < `tiprail` < `plain` < `castle` by hanging thread. `tiprail` should
   beat that ranking, because its rail is laid after the teeth and the gate scores the
   teeth as they fall; if the ring-beam idea is right, the finished sector is stiffer
   than its count says. A method that beats its own gate count is a finding about the
   gate, not only about the method.

4. **`plain` may still win on stiffness despite a high count**, because it is the only
   K1 shape whose two paths are each laid six times onto themselves. If it does, then
   stiffness comes from self-stacking and not from the amount of unsupported thread, and
   the model of the problem has to be rewritten. This is the DOMET-versus-ziggurat
   question, asked on one object at one reach on one day.

5. **Each rung matches its copy 180 degrees away.** The gate cannot see the fan, so the
   two copies score identically; if the printed pair differs, part cooling is a
   first-order term and every azimuthal ladder printed so far, DOMET included, has to be
   re-read.

6. Nothing here predicts adhesion, warping, or that the print completes. The gate does
   not simulate the machine.
