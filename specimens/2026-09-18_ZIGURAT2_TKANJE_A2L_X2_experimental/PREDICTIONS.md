# PREDICTIONS — ZIGURAT2 TKANJE · A2L · X2 (written 2026-09-18, before any print)

The A2L's bead (0.45 mm) is measured. This is the first WEFT object whose steps are made in mid-air: only the
lowest tier stands on the plate, and every terrace above it is a corner cut — a 45° chord across each corner,
grown 0.5 mm per layer until the four cuts meet at the edge midpoints and the square has become a square rotated
45° with side a/√2. Sides run 175.6 → 124.2 → 87.8 → 62.1 → 43.9 mm; 192 × 192 × 121 mm, 503 layers, 500 m of
thread, 67.5 g.

The evidence behind the method: OBLAK's upper hemisphere is a corbel over a 254 mm void and printed clean until
its per-layer step passed ~0.9 mm (photographs, 8 September). Here the step is 0.5 mm, and it is gentler than a
uniform corbel because a cut chord's ends land on solid material and only its middle overhangs.

Falsifiers: a ring dropping anywhere in a cut (then 0.5 mm/layer is too fast for a 45° chord and the rate must
come down); the first tier's brim lifting in the first hour; the crown's first chord breaking rather than sagging.

What this print is for: it is the first object in the record whose horizontal transitions are made by geometry
alone — no bridges, no support, nothing on the plate but the bottom step. If it stands, the corner cut becomes
the qualified way to step a WEFT form inward, and the terrace-as-bridge idea (X1) can be retired.
