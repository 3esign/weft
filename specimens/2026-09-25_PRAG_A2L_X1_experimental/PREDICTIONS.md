# PRAG — predictions, written before the print

Stated so that the print can contradict them.

1. **The 3 and 6 mm sectors come out as flat shelves** on both machines: their free span is inside the
   range every woven wall already survives.
2. **The 18 mm sectors curl or nest**, and the 13 mm sectors are marginal. The break lies between 9
   and 13 mm. If instead all five rungs hold, the free span at the tip is not the variable that made
   KRAK a hook, and KUKA's answer (the free end) is the one that matters.
3. **A rung matches its twin 180° away.** If it does not, part cooling is a first-order term and
   every azimuthal ladder printed so far has to be re-read with that in mind.
4. **The drum is untouched whatever the terrace does.** If the terrace tangles and the print is stopped,
   the object below is complete and readable — that is the design, and it is itself a prediction.
5. **The median free reach of the terrace's tips (20.7 mm to solid material) does not by itself
   destroy the object**, because a corbel of 2.5 mm steps is not a pile of 24 mm cantilevers even if
   the instrument measures the same distance for both. If it does destroy the object, the reach-to-solid
   number is the right one for corbels too and the hold limit for corbels is below 22 mm.
6. Nothing here predicts adhesion or that the print completes.
