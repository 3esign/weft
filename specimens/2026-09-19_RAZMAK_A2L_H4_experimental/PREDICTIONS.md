# RAZMAK — predictions before printing

Status: designed and generated; no new physical result. Four wall levels, three horizontal terraces and a closed patterned roof. This is an exploratory sculpture, not a controlled comparison against OBRTAJ or the older X2 prints.

The visual idea is a sequence of wide pauses: a rounded square base, an off-centre hexagon, a differently displaced rounded square and a curved crown. Tall transparent walls leave the horizontal carrying surfaces exposed. The first terrace reaches almost the entire 300 × 290 mm allowed XY envelope; the full design rises to 309.84 mm inside the 310 mm ceiling.

## What changes from the physical X2 baseline

Only the basal perimeter frame touches the bed. Each later wall begins on a terrace at its stated height. A terrace occupies the same annular footprint for all twelve layers: it is not a sloping corner-cut transition. Each returning line crosses the supporting wall and projects inward and outward. Successive layers shift by one quarter of a cell and have independently checked nominal intersections. Return count is limited by the space at the inner edge, not solely by the larger supporting perimeter.

The outer projection is the proposed counterweight/restraint mechanism. A continuous strand turns around a free tip and returns to the old wall; the gate can label this run a bridge although mechanically it behaves as a projecting loop. Its two supported endpoints do not prove that the outward mass produces useful prestress. There are no outer support pillars and no inner bed-founded tubes.

## Unknowns retained

The useful inward/outward projection ratio; whether thermal contraction tensions or destabilizes the loops; actual bonding at nominal crossings; how deformation propagates after higher walls are added; influence of cooling and material; nozzle interaction with raised or slack strands; whether the dense roof forms as intended. None has been measured by this build. No numerical sag or strength prediction is asserted.

## Observation record

Use the G-code hash as the print identity. Record material/colour/batch, drying state if known, actual temperatures/speed overrides, start/end time and any intervention. Before the next wall begins, photograph each terrace from the same fixed side position with a scale; repeat after the next wall and at completion. Keep +X/−X/+Y/−Y directions identifiable. Record first visible bowing, missing contact, curling, nozzle contact and interruption with layer/Z. After removal, photograph the underside and compare its height with the intended terrace plane. Weigh the specimen if practical.

The first follow-up should keep this geometry and printer settings fixed and change one routing variable, such as phase or return order. The second sculpture is a separate challenge, not a machine or texture control group. No programmed pauses or printer upload are included.

Honest verdict: design intent and falsifiable observations are specified. Geometric checks cannot establish useful counterweight action, bond quality, reduced sag or print completion.
