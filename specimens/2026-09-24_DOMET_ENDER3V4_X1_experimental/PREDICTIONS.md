# DOMET — predictions, and how to read the object

Status: designed and generated; no physical result. This is a controlled instrument, not a sculpture.

## What is predicted

1. **Terrace A** will be a clean comb in its short sectors and will fail somewhere in its long ones. The prior
   evidence says the break should fall between 20 and 26 mm on the Ender and lower on the A2L. If A is clean
   all the way to 26 mm, the ribs bought more than the bracket suggested and the next ladder must start higher.
2. **Terrace B** (no ribs, two paths, the 2026-09-19 scheme) should break at a **shorter** reach than A. If B
   and A break at the same sector, the ribs bought nothing and the whole rib idea is decoration — that is the
   single most useful thing this object can tell us, and it is the outcome that would hurt most.
3. **Terrace C** (inward) may break earlier than A at the same nominal reach, because an inward tip has no wall
   mass outboard of it and because the fan converges, so teeth had to be dropped (63 cells against 80). If C
   is *better* than A, the counterweight story told about the outward return on 2026-09-19 is wrong.
4. **Terrace D** holds the reach at 20 mm and varies the rib pitch. The expectation is a threshold: fine pitches
   indistinguishable from each other, then a pitch beyond which the terrace behaves like the ribless sector.
   The eighth sector has no ribs at all and is the in-terrace control.

None of this is a numerical sag prediction. No strength claim is made.

## How to read it

The eight sectors ascend clockwise from the ladder's shortest rung. The break shows as the angle at which the
comb stops being a comb: tips curl, then neighbouring tips touch, then the thread lifts and the nozzle drags it.
Record, per terrace, **the sector in which the first curl appears and the sector in which the surface is lost.**

- Photograph each terrace from a fixed side position with a scale, before the wall above it starts and after.
- Identify +X/−X/+Y/−Y so a sector can be named from a photograph.
- Photograph the underside after removal; the growth cone and any sag show there and nowhere else.
- Weigh the object. The header predicts its mass; a large discrepancy means the bead is wrong, which on the
  Ender it may well be — that machine has never been bead-calibrated.
- Keep a failure. An interrupted DOMET is worth more than a finished one that tells us nothing.

## What would disprove the design rather than the reach

If findings appear in a **wall** rather than a terrace, the instrument is wrong, not the hypothesis. If the
first terrace layer fails uniformly at every reach including 5 mm, the base ring is not seating and the fault
is in how the terrace meets the wall, not in the hairpin.

Honest verdict: the test regions and the falsifying observations are specified. A digital gate cannot say
whether hot plastic stays horizontal.
